## Establish a common basis

Record the candidate, version, owner, reviewer, currency, price date, geographic scope, covered population, service units, start and end dates, and evidence references. Show monthly cash timing through the trial and funded closure, with annual summaries over a justified multi-year horizon. Extend the horizon for debt, replacement, guarantees, or other obligations that outlast the trial.

Keep nominal cash flows and inflation assumptions explicit. If presenting constant-price comparisons, label them and reconcile to nominal payment needs. Document exchange rates, settlement dates, and currency risks for any cross-border obligation. Unknown inputs remain unknown; a blank field is not zero.

## Register every funding source

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and authority | Source, payer, responsible public body where applicable, agreement or authority reference, approval status, and evidence date. |
| Availability | Amount, currency, expected receipt dates, restrictions, conditions, expiry, and whether funds are received, committed subject to conditions, or merely proposed. |
| Durability | Recurring or temporary; renewal assumptions; concentration by payer; collection risk and delay. |
| Net contribution | Collection and administration costs, associated delivery obligations, and usable net cash. |
| Review | Owner, independent reviewer, conflicts, next review date, and unresolved questions. |

Use only funds available for the relevant purpose and period in the funded case. Show conditional commitments separately until conditions are satisfied; model collection delays even for confirmed sources. Do not count restricted grants as unrestricted reserves, borrowing as recurring income, asset sales as recurring revenue, or hypothetical future AI gains as committed receipts.

Utility or investment proceeds must be net of their own obligations. Do not count a utility surplus and the dividend paid from that same surplus twice. Show investment losses and payout restrictions; no dependable dividend is assumed.

## Cost the service and the transition

Tie quantities to T02 definitions and T03 deliverable capacity. For each cost record quantity, unit price, timing, payer, evidence, confidence, fixed or variable behavior, and how it changes with enrollment.

Include service delivery, staff and administration, enrollment and assisted access, complaints and appeals, evaluation, independent audit, maintenance, data protection, supplier switching, contingency operations, and funded closure. Show startup equipment and construction, replacement schedules, debt principal and interest, and any existing-benefit compensation separately. Identify unpaid or donated inputs and their replacement cost without inserting them as cash payments unless payable.

Separate cash from accrual expenses: depreciation may explain asset consumption but is not an additional cash payment on top of the same capital purchase. Record liabilities when incurred as well as their payment dates. Deferred bills must not create an appearance of affordability.

## Reconcile cash and reserves

Maintain opening unrestricted operating cash, actual or committed usable receipts, external cash payments, reserve transfers, and closing balances for each period. Track restricted funds in separate schedules.

Operating closing cash = operating opening cash + usable receipts + authorized reserve transfers in - external payments - transfers to reserves.

Reserve closing cash = reserve opening cash + transfers from operations - transfers to operations.

A transfer between these two accounts cancels in consolidated unrestricted cash. Count an external payment once. Reserve balances pledged, legally restricted, or unavailable on the required date cannot finance operating gaps.

Publish three distinct results:

- Cash balance and payment timing, including the lowest balance within the period.
- Recurring operating coverage: usable recurring revenue divided by recurring service, administration, and maintenance costs. A zero denominator is not an infinite coverage result; explain applicability.
- Full funding sufficiency: all startup, capital, debt, reserve, continuity, and other obligations, including commitments beyond the operating horizon.

A recurring coverage ratio above one does not establish full funding sufficiency. Finite reserves can bridge a temporary gap but cannot demonstrate permanent affordability. Set a locally reviewed minimum reserve, permitted uses, approval authority, replenishment plan, and protected closure allocation. Report when those protections would be breached.

## Compare financing options on the same service scope

Compare a baseline using existing provision, a bounded additional-service proposal, and alternative funding mixes. Possible sources to assess include existing authorized revenue, land or resource revenue, pollution charges, infrastructure charges, grants, and negotiated investment returns. Each option needs evidence and qualified local review; inclusion here is not endorsement of a tax or funding instrument.

For each option show gross and net receipts, legal and collection feasibility, implementation time, volatility, administration cost, household and business effects, and distribution across affected groups. Identify who initially pays and who may ultimately bear costs through prices, rents, wages, or reduced services. Publish uncertainty rather than treating pass-through assumptions as measured facts.

Record capital and public guarantees separately, including maximum exposure, triggers, and who bears a loss. Borrowing needs repayment, refinancing, interest-rate, and currency analysis. Do not propose reducing existing taxes until replacement funding and physical service capacity pass the required independent review.

## Exercise shocks

Begin with the architecture's illustrative 20% recurring-revenue decline and 25% essentials-cost increase. These are scenarios, not forecasts. Specify duration, timing, affected cost lines, and recovery assumptions; also test them together.

Add delayed receipts, supplier loss, enrollment growth, outage, and sponsor withdrawal. Link physical disruption and switching lead times to T03. For each scenario publish monthly cash, service gaps, reserve draw, first protected-balance breach, remaining continuity funds, corrective owner, and decision trigger. A financially balanced case can still fail physical delivery.

### Fictional arithmetic check

All values below are invented currency units per month. They represent a simplified recurring operation with 100 in service costs and 20 in administration and maintenance. Capital, debt, closure, and other obligations are excluded and require separate schedules.

| Scenario | Recurring revenue | Service cost | Other recurring cost | Monthly surplus or gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Base | 130 | 100 | 20 | 10 |
| Revenue down 20% | 104 | 100 | 20 | -16 |
| Service cost up 25% | 130 | 125 | 20 | -15 |
| Combined | 104 | 125 | 20 | -41 |

With 180 of unrestricted reserves and a protected minimum of 60, only 120 is available to bridge operating gaps. Under a constant combined monthly gap of 41, that margin lasts about 2.93 months: two complete months, with the minimum breached during month three. Actual payment timing could cause an earlier breach. The calculation is not evidence that the trial is funded.
