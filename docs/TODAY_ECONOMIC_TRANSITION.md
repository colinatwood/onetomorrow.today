# Today economic transition architecture and delivery backlog

Status: proposed expansion of economic architecture 3.4.1. Prepared 18 October 2026. This is the Today branch of OneTomorrow.Today. Tomorrow owns agency and human–AI symbiosis; Future owns Ark development and exploration.

Today aims to secure essential needs, preserve room for enterprise, reduce environmental harm, and make economic institutions accountable. Begin within existing national systems and expand through publicly authorized local trials, regional cooperation, and agreements between participating countries. The transition must work without assuming AGI, unlimited resources, perfect networks, or universal political agreement.

The public explanation is in [today.html](../today.html). This document defines proposed operating requirements and the work needed to evaluate them. It does not establish legal rights, commit public funds, operate benefits, or claim that partners have agreed to participate.

## Changes from architecture 3.4.1

| Earlier proposal | Treatment in the expanded architecture |
| --- | --- |
| Absolute human survival floor | Retain universal access as the goal; specify services, delivery duties, capacity, financing, and shortage procedures. Describe pilot coverage accurately. |
| Frictionless commercial sandbox | Simplify business formation and reporting while retaining competition, labor, safety, consumer, and environmental accountability. |
| Stationary Asset Tax and mandatory annual deployment | Preserve as design history. Neither mandatory 35% spending nor a 24-month reserve penalty is a requirement of the transition. |
| Universal public corporate equity | Compare negotiated public investment, community ownership, and worker ownership. Do not assume compulsory stakes or dependable dividends. |
| Local value guilds | Test accountable service organizations with defined authority, representation, open fees, audits, and appeals. Compare organizational forms. |
| Zero personal and corporate income taxes | Retain as a scenario to evaluate. Existing revenue continues until a credible replacement is authorized and proven. |
| Fully inflation-proof resource ledger | Replace with optional resource accounts specifying location, quality, timing, capacity, redemption liability, and failure procedures. No immunity to scarcity is claimed. |
| Automated geographic valuation without human review | Use explainable assistance, independent validation, correction, and appeals. Automated assessment cannot eliminate speculation by declaration. |
| Citizen endowment votes | Test discretionary allocations after essential obligations and reserves are funded. Protect minority rights from majority allocation decisions. |

## Institutional transition

Countries differ in fiscal capacity, public trust, administrative capability, currencies, and economic structure. National institutions can support both competition and cooperation; identify specific failures rather than treating every national system as identical.

Local organizations adapt delivery to residents. Regional institutions coordinate shared grids, housing markets, transport, and supply. National institutions authorize revenue, rights, fiscal transfers, and public liabilities. Agreements between countries cover common reporting, research access, procurement, emergency reserves, and portable services where agreed.

Every agreement identifies its public authority, contributions, beneficiaries, settlement currency, currency risk, audit rules, dispute forum, emergency exceptions, and exit obligations. Residents need continuity if a government or provider withdraws. A single global treasury and mandatory global currency are not prerequisites.

Participation should deliver reliable supply, lower delivery costs, shared research, and access to markets. Negotiate contributions reflecting capacity and need. Include lower-income regions and affected Indigenous communities in decisions about resources, land, and technology. Assess whether gains shift exploitation, debt, extraction, or pollution elsewhere.

Review pensions, debt, property interests, contracts, and trade commitments before institutional change. Public asset transfers require valuation, disclosure, authorization, and analysis of who gains or loses. Do not finance recurring entitlements with hypothetical future AI revenue.

## Essential needs and freedom

Define a locally costed basket covering nutrition, water, sanitation, secure shelter, basic energy, essential healthcare, and connectivity. Adjust for children, household size, disability, medical needs, and climate. Universal coverage is the destination; each trial publishes its population, duration, limits, and expansion path.

Specify access for residents, newcomers, refugees, undocumented people, and people without digital access. Provide emergency access, assisted enrollment, proportionate checks, privacy safeguards, and appeals. Voluntary pilot participation does not turn equal rights or legal obligations into optional platform terms.

Protect minimum access from sale, collateralization, political loyalty tests, employment requirements, and unreviewable automated termination. Use cash where appropriate and capacity investment where bottlenecks dominate. Design benefit withdrawal rules, where applicable, to avoid abrupt losses when someone accepts paid work.

Publish shortage priorities, reserves, supplier redundancy, emergency purchasing authority, and mutual aid arrangements. Establish accountable response and continuity duties without promising physical availability through every catastrophe.

## Funding and physical accounting

Publish annual cash budgets and a multi-year liability view. Separate recurring committed revenue from temporary grants, investment gains, and proposed revenue; operating expenditure from capital and financing cash flows; restricted funds from money available for the general floor; and private obligations from public guarantees.

For every period, committed receipts plus an authorized available reserve draw must cover service costs, administration, maintenance, capital and debt cash obligations, and required reserve contributions. Show opening and closing cash. Avoid double-counting depreciation and replacement in a cash budget, or dividends and the same underlying utility surplus as separate revenue.

Also test recurring coverage without reserve draws: reserves are finite. Publish gaps, runway, and corrective authority. Compare existing taxes, land and resource revenues, pollution charges, negotiated investment returns, and infrastructure charges. No rate is settled in advance. Assess incidence, including fees passed into household prices.

Count utility receipts after operating, maintenance, replacement, and financing obligations. Borrowing requires repayment and currency-risk analysis; an endowment needs investment governance, realistic payout rules, and loss scenarios. Reduce existing taxes only after replacement funding and service capacity withstand independently reviewed stress tests.

For each resource, verify deliverable output plus usable stocks and contracted imports, less exports, losses, and reserve requirements, against obligations by location and time. Financial solvency cannot substitute for physical delivery.

Future utility exchanges require capacity, conversion and transport costs, settlement responsibility, redemption priorities, expiry rules where appropriate, and failed-delivery procedures. Electricity cannot become food without production and logistics. Test exchanges in a nonfinancial simulation before considering real use; protect minimum entitlements from trading.

## Work and sustainability

Track realized technology adoption, earnings, hours, vacancies, job quality, and bargaining power. The ILO's 2025 research distinguishes occupational exposure from realized job loss and emphasizes job transformation [1]. Prepare for slow, uneven, and rapid automation.

Compare paid training linked to vacancies, portable benefits, temporary income support, worker ownership, and access to finance. Recognize unpaid care and disability. Evaluate whether subsidies favor incumbents or displace other workers. Essentials must not depend on accepting an AI-directed job.

Measure total emissions, material use, water stress, land and biodiversity impacts, and waste, including imports and AI infrastructure. UNEP identifies resource pressures and pathways for more sustainable production and consumption [3]. Check efficiency gains against total consumption and local ecological limits. Include repair, reuse, maintenance, and restoration.

Support open research consistent with licenses. Finance open-source maintenance through grants, procurement, service contracts, and voluntary contributions rather than compulsory metering of all knowledge use.

## Accountability and recovery

Publish plain-language budgets and machine-readable funding, procurement, payments, outcomes, and correction records. The IMF framework covers reporting, budgeting, risk, and resource revenues [2]; OCDS supplies a public-contracting data model [5]. Independent verification and accessible challenge procedures accompany publication.

Protect household and medical data. Minimize collection, separate identity from public analytics, define retention, assess reidentification risk, and publish safe aggregates. Explain justified redactions.

Guild and endowment governance needs resident and worker representation, conflicts disclosure, term and removal rules, separation of operator and auditor, appeals, and safeguards against capture by funders, officials, or token holders. Discretionary voting cannot remove the essential floor or override rights.

AI may assist forecasting and anomaly detection. Retain named accountable decision owners, published methods and errors, change logs, human correction, manual operations, and tested backups. Essential access must survive a lost phone, identity dispute, connectivity loss, compromised operator, or payment failure. The World Bank's adaptive protection framework treats programs, information, finance, and institutions as complementary requirements [4].

## Phases and gates

| Phase | Indicative horizon from authorized start | Evidence before advancing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline and design | First 90 days | Sponsor, authority, consultation, baseline, costed scope, committed funds, rights and privacy plan, independent evaluation and stop rules. |
| Bounded trial | Months 4–24 | Comparison against baseline and an appropriate counterfactual; better access; published full costs; funded continuity; no unresolved serious rights or safety failure. |
| Regional replication | Years 2–5 | Independent replication; capacity and funding survive stress; shared reporting, settlement, and provider-exit rules. |
| Wider cooperation | Years 5 onward if warranted | Public authorization; fair contributions; settlement and dispute rules; documented shared benefits; no displaced social or environmental costs. |

Horizons are planning assumptions, not launch commitments. A jurisdiction can remain at a phase, revise, or stop expansion. Closure implements funded continuity rather than abruptly withdrawing support.

## Scorecard and stress tests

Before launch, set locally justified thresholds for unmet needs, household costs, reliability, funding coverage, reserves, worker outcomes, concentration, environmental totals, enrollment errors, and complaint resolution. Publish definitions, denominators, uncertainty, and safe subgroup results. Do not let aggregate gains conceal worsening exclusion.

Account for overlapping programs and full operating and transition costs. Use a justified comparison method; a randomized design may be useful where ethical and feasible, otherwise explain the alternative and attribution limits.

Start by testing a 20% recurring revenue decline, 25% essentials cost increase, loss of the largest supplier, regional power and connectivity outage, payment failure, and sudden enrollment growth. These are illustrative stress assumptions, not forecasts; calibrate severity and duration to local evidence and test combined shocks. Record response authority, reserve runway, unmet obligations, and resident protections.
