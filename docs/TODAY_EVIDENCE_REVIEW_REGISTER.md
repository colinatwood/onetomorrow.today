## Review record

For each public claim, record its page and section, exact claim, evidence reference, observation period, last verification date, reviewer, next review date, and status. Reviewer and dates remain unassigned until a person accepts the work; the preparation date above is not a verification date.

Use these statuses: supported for the stated period; proposal; awaiting verification; stale; disputed; withdrawn. Keep operational delivery status separate from evidence status. A well-supported proposal is still a proposal.

## Triggers and corrections

Review after a source revision, expired supplier commitment, changed coverage, correction request, funding change, or missed review date. A missed date changes evidence to awaiting verification; it does not prove that the underlying claim is false. Escalate essential-service readiness claims immediately to the accountable owner and suspend unsupported readiness language pending review.

Record the previous wording, correction, reason, source, reviewer and publication date. Update the affected Today section, public status entry and topic description together where they repeat the claim. Retain a visible correction history without personal or sensitive evidence. Explain unresolved disagreements and uncertainty.

## Acceptance for the first maintenance cycle

An assigned editor inventories current readiness claims, enters actual review dates and owners, reconciles worksheet links with the architecture backlog, and checks that public statuses do not imply selected partners, funding or operation. An independent reviewer confirms the changed claims against dated sources. This blank procedure does not complete that cycle.
