Governance

Trust requires visible rules for changing the work.

OneTomorrow is an open proposal maintained through its public source repository. This page explains who decides what appears here, how evidence is labeled, and how anyone can challenge a mistake.

Policy version: 1.1 · October 19, 2026

Who maintains the site

The repository owner and approved contributors maintain the published site. A named program owner is not implied unless the status page lists one.

How decisions are made

Changes should identify the problem, affected people, evidence, risks, alternatives, and a reversible first step. Material changes receive public review through a pull request before publication.

Conflicts of interest

Authors and reviewers should disclose financial, employment, political, or personal interests that could reasonably affect a proposal. Undisclosed conflicts are grounds for review or removal.

Why proposals are declined

A proposal may be declined when it cannot be explained plainly, lacks a responsible owner, hides important costs, removes meaningful consent, creates avoidable harm, or presents speculation as established fact.

Evidence labels

Separate what is known from what is imagined.

Established evidence

Supported by reliable, relevant sources or repeatable observations. The source and review date should be visible.

Working hypothesis

A testable idea whose outcome is uncertain. It needs success and failure thresholds before a pilot begins.

Value position

An ethical or political choice, such as protecting consent or sharing public benefits. It should not be disguised as a scientific finding.

Corrections

Challenge the record in public.

Use the guided correction form for factual errors, broken sources, inaccessible language, undisclosed conflicts, or misleading status claims. Email join@onetomorrow.today when the report must remain private.

  1. The maintainer acknowledges a specific report.
  2. The disputed text and supporting sources are reviewed.
  3. The text is corrected, clarified, or retained with a public explanation.
  4. Material corrections are recorded in repository history and linked from this page.
