The 20-Watt Mind: governing the silicon age.

Advanced AI uses large amounts of computing power and electricity. The human brain handles vision, reasoning, and many senses at once using about twenty watts—the power of a dim household bulb. Education should build on the brain's ability to understand context efficiently.

Clean power is the operating system of a free future. Clean minds are the operating system of a free AI future. OneTomorrow treats captured cognition the way it treats captured budgets: as corruption to be designed out.

The core paradox

For a century, schools trained humans to be poor, inefficient hard drives.

Standardized education often rewards memorizing facts. Machines can now store and compare far more information than a person. If students rely only on AI-generated answers, they may also learn to think in the same narrow patterns. Education should instead strengthen judgment, curiosity, creativity, and the ability to question a machine's answer.

The human energy advantage

Stop rewarding memorization. Start building judgment.

The learning approach proposed here replaces large amounts of memorized material with a smaller set of useful ideas that work across subjects. Learners practice applying those ideas to difficult real-world situations and guiding AI, rather than trying to remember more facts than a machine.

  • The 20-watt brain. Build on the brain's strength at using experience, physical senses, and context while consuming very little energy.
  • Keep what matters. Teach core ideas and reusable ways of thinking instead of memorization for its own sake.
  • Question the machine. Teach people to pause, check, challenge, and redirect an AI answer.

Charter article XI

Control over how we think and learn.

This principle—sometimes called cognitive sovereignty—sits beside digital rights in the charter. Each promise below must be public, open to inspection, and possible to verify locally.

Right to govern

Every child is educated to steer intelligent systems, not to compete with them on recall.

Learning methods stay public

Core teaching methods are open source and available to everyone. No company may take exclusive control of them.

Data stays home

Learner records stay in separate, protected national storage under regional control. Each nation can refuse to let that data leave the country.

Judgment over memorization

The ability to question and guide AI is accepted as an alternative way to show learning.

No monoculture

Communities choose their own cultural and ethical anchors. The global layer supplies bridges, never content.

AI people can inspect

Any AI used by a local learning group must clearly show how it works and how it reaches decisions.

Three tiers, one rule

Local matters locally. National matters nationally. Global only where necessary.

Article II of the charter already sets this rule for government. The learning plan applies it to schools, so growth never gives one central organization control over everyone.

The local learning kit

Give communities a ready-to-use toolkit.

OneTomorrow does not wait for every national law to change. It gives open-source kits directly to regional leaders, small schools, and families who teach at home.

  • Anchors. Each community selects its own cultural, ethical, and local-knowledge foundations.
  • Bridges. Shared open-source software lets any local group connect to AI tools without depending on one central server.
  • Loops. A common exchange method lets local groups share learning across subjects directly and safely.

Horizon 4

Steady state

Years 16-20 · Plan phase 4

Systemic state

  • The nonprofit decentralizes its core staff footprint.
  • Regions, nations, and communities run the network together without one central owner.
  • Human minds are natively trained to guide, critique, and balance high-throughput AI for the survival and flourishing of their own communities.

Signals

  • The shared network supports itself without central staff.
  • Learner data sovereignty at or near 100 percent.
  • Concept-steering mastery a recognized credential worldwide.
